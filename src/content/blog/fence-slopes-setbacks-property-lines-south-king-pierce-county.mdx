## Start with the grade, not the panel

Before we quote a single panel, we walk the full fence line and note where the ground rises, dips or breaks. That tells us which of the two slope methods each run needs.

### Stepped panels

**Stepped** panels stay level and drop in stages, like a staircase. They suit steep or uneven grades and keep the clean, square look of a privacy fence. The trade-off is a triangular gap under each step, which we close with a kickboard, grading or a taller panel where privacy or pets matter.

### Racked panels

**Racked** panels tilt to follow the ground, so the bottom stays close to the grade with no stair-step gaps. They work best on steady, gentle slopes. Because our cedar panels are built before install day, we decide stepped vs. racked during planning, not in the field.

### Mixing both

Many yards in [Auburn](/service-areas/auburn), [Kent](/service-areas/kent) and [Federal Way](/service-areas/federal-way) need both: racked along a gentle side yard, stepped where the back of the lot drops off. Our [Fence Genius](/fence-genius) planning shows you that layout before posts go in.

## Setbacks: rules change at every city line

The South Sound is a patchwork of cities and unincorporated county land, and each one writes its own fence rules: height limits, street-side and corner-lot sight areas, and where a front-yard fence has to stay lower. We don't guess. Before we build, we check the code for your exact address:

- **Auburn:** [Auburn City Code](https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/Auburn/)
- **Kent:** [Kent City Code](https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/Kent/)
- **Federal Way:** [Federal Way Revised Code](https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/FederalWay/)
- **Puyallup:** [Puyallup Municipal Code](https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/Puyallup/) (see also our [Puyallup fence permit guide](/blog/do-you-need-a-permit-for-a-fence-in-puyallup-height-setbacks-rules))
- **Tacoma:** [Tacoma Municipal Code](https://www.cityoftacoma.org/government/city_departments/city_attorney/city_clerk/tacoma_municipal_code) and [Tacoma Permits](https://www.tacomapermits.org/)
- **Unincorporated King County:** [King County Code](https://kingcounty.gov/en/legacy/council/legislation/kc_code)
- **Unincorporated Pierce County:** [Pierce County Code](https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/PierceCounty/) and [Pierce County Planning & Public Works](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/903/Planning-Public-Works)

HOAs add another layer. Many neighborhoods in Auburn, Kent and Pierce County have their own rules on height, style and stain color, so check your CC&Rs too. Pre-stained cedar makes it easy to match an approved color across the whole fence.

## Property lines: know where the line is

A fence post set a foot onto your neighbor's land can turn into years of disputes. Here's how to find the line before you build:

1. **Check your records.** Look up your parcel through [King County property research](https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/local-services/buildings-property/property-research-and-maps/property-research) or [Pierce County's assessor tools](https://atip.piercecountywa.gov/). Parcel maps are a starting point, not a survey.
2. **Find the survey markers.** If you can't locate the corner markers, or they disagree with an old fence, hire a licensed land surveyor.
3. **Talk to your neighbor.** A shared boundary fence goes much smoother when both sides agree on the line and the plan. Washington's fence law is in [RCW 16.60](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=16.60).
4. **If you disagree, put it in writing.** Under [RCW 58.04.007](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=58.04.007), landowners can fix a disputed boundary with a signed, recorded agreement that includes a survey map. If they can't agree, any of them can ask a court to decide.

Many homeowners also choose to set the fence a little inside their own line. That leaves room for maintenance and avoids arguments over a few inches.

## Call 811 before anyone digs

Washington requires a utility locate before digging. Request one through [Washington 811](https://www.washington811.com/) so gas, power and water lines are marked before post holes go in. Slopes make this even more important, because buried lines don't always follow the grade.

## Hire a licensed, bonded contractor

Confirm your fence contractor is registered with [Washington L&I](https://www.lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors). On hillside work, ask them to explain their post depth, how they handle grade changes, and who checks setbacks. Those answers show who planned the job and who is planning to wing it.

## FAQ

### Should my fence be stepped or racked on a slope?
Steady, gentle grades usually suit racked panels. Steeper or uneven grades usually suit stepped panels. Many South Sound yards use both on different runs.

### Where do I find the fence rules for my city?
Auburn, Kent, Federal Way and Puyallup publish their codes on Code Publishing, Tacoma publishes its code on the city website, and unincorporated areas follow the King County or Pierce County code (all linked above). Rules vary by city and zone, so confirm with your city's permit counter.

### Can I build my fence right on the property line?
Only if you know exactly where the line is. If it's unclear, get a licensed survey. If neighbors disagree, RCW 58.04.007 lays out how to settle it.

### Do I need to call 811 before installing a fence?
Yes. Request a locate through Washington 811 before anyone digs.

### Why choose pre-stained, pre-built cedar panels for a sloped yard?
The panels are built and stained before install day, so the crew spends its time on layout, post depth and grade transitions, and you skip the staining step afterward.
