Most "best time to install a fence" advice is written for places where the ground freezes solid and summers bake. Puget Sound is different. Our problem isn't frost or heat. It's **water**: months of steady rain, damp air and short, gray days. This guide uses real climate data for the Seattle area to show what actually limits fence work in each season. It also explains why a **pre-stained, pre-built cedar privacy fence** changes the answer to "should I wait until spring?"

<h2 id="short-answer">The short answer</h2>

If you want the driest weather, **late spring through early fall** is the easiest stretch for any outdoor project around Seattle. July and August are the driest months on record at Sea-Tac.

But "driest" isn't the same as "only." Light rain on its own doesn't stop a well-run crew from setting posts and hanging panels. What wet months mainly make hard is **putting a finish on raw wood outdoors**. When the boards arrive already dried and stained on every side, and the panels are built in a shop, most of that problem goes away.

<Callout title="The bottom line" variant="info">
For a cedar privacy fence, the season matters most if the wood will be stained on site after it's built. If it arrives pre-stained and pre-built, the main wet-season limits are things like heavy downpours, standing water in post holes and short daylight, and those usually cost days on the schedule, not quality.
</Callout>

<h2 id="seattle-is-different">Why Seattle's calendar is different</h2>

Generic guides from Chicago, New England or the Midwest tell you to avoid winter because the ground freezes. NOAA's 1991–2020 climate normals for Seattle-Tacoma International Airport tell a different story ([NOAA NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&format=pdf&stations=USW00024233&units=standard)):

- **Days that never get above freezing are rare.** Sea-Tac averages about **1.1 days a year** with a high of 32°F or lower.
- **Freezing nights happen, but not often.** It averages about **25 nights a year** at or below 32°F, most of them in December and January.
- **Rain is the real constraint.** Sea-Tac averages **39.34 inches** of precipitation a year, and November alone averages **6.31 inches**, with measurable rain on about **18 days**.

So around Puget Sound, the question isn't "is the ground frozen?" It's "how wet is it, and what does that do to the wood and the finish?"

<Callout title="Your yard isn't the airport" variant="warning">
These numbers come from one weather station at Sea-Tac. Lots up on the Sammamish Plateau, in the Cascade foothills or right on the water can be cooler, wetter or windier. Use the table as a guide to the pattern, not a forecast for your street.
</Callout>

### How to read the table

- **May through September** is the dry, warm stretch. It's also when everyone else wants their fence built.
- **October and April** are shoulder months: wetter, but most days still reach 50°F.
- **November through February** is the true wet season. December averages only **8.5 days** that reach 50°F, and wood in the outdoor air sits near its wettest point of the year.

Keep that 50°F column in mind. It matters a lot for staining, as you'll see below.

<h2 id="what-rain-affects">What wet weather really affects</h2>

Rain affects each stage of a fence project differently. Here's an honest breakdown.

### Digging and setting posts

Post holes can be dug in wet soil. The issues are practical: muddy access across lawns, holes that collect water, and saturated ground on low corners and downhill lots. Ask any contractor how they handle **standing water in a post hole** and what the label on their post-setting product says about wet or cold conditions. A good crew will push a day rather than set posts in a flooded hole.

Whatever the season, Washington's dig law still applies. The Washington Utilities and Transportation Commission says to call **811** or request a locate online **at least two business days before digging** ([WA UTC](https://www.utc.wa.gov/public-safety/pipeline-safety/call-you-dig)). The homeowner exemption only covers digging **less than 12 inches** on noncommercial property ([WA UTC](https://www.utc.wa.gov/washington-dig-law-realtors)), and fence post holes are deeper than that.

### Wood movement

Wood swells as it takes on moisture and shrinks as it dries. The *Wood Handbook* advises installing wood **at the moisture content it will experience in service** to keep dimensional changes after installation small ([USDA FPL](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/62261.pdf)). Its Seattle figures range from about **12.0% in July to 16.9% in December**.

In plain terms: boards that go up soaking wet will keep moving as they dry, and boards that go up bone dry in August will take on moisture in November. Wood that starts out dried to a moderate, controlled level, close to what it will see in our air, has less adjusting to do once it's on the fence.

<h2 id="staining-is-the-bottleneck">Why on-site staining is the real bottleneck</h2>

Every exterior stain has weather limits printed on its data sheet. They vary by product, so always follow the label for the one you use. Here are two examples from the same brand:

- Flood's semi-transparent **alkyd/oil** stain says to apply when **no precipitation is expected for 24 hours**, and when air and surface temperatures are **50°F to 90°F and will stay above 50°F for the next 24 hours** ([Flood product data sheet](https://buyat.ppg.com/rep_pafpainttools_files/Flood/tdb/FLD802.pdf)).
- Flood's semi-transparent **acrylic/oil** stain allows a lower range, down to **35°F**. It still calls for **no precipitation for 24 hours**, and it warns that high humidity or temperatures below 50°F may prolong drying ([Flood product data sheet](https://buyat.ppg.com/rep_pafpainttools_files/Flood/tdb/FLD812.pdf)).

Both labels also say the surface must be **clean and dry** before you start.

Now lay that over the Sea-Tac numbers. In December, the average month has measurable rain on about **18 days**, and only about **8.5 days** reach 50°F at all ([NOAA NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&format=pdf&stations=USW00024233&units=standard)). Finding a full 24-hour window that's dry, warm enough and comes after the boards have dried out is genuinely hard from November through February.

<Callout title="What that means for a raw-cedar fence built in winter" variant="warning">
If raw cedar goes up in the wet season and the plan is to stain it on site, the finish usually has to wait. In the meantime the wood is exposed to months of rain. And even when you do stain, the back of the boards, the faces that sit against rails and the cut ends are hard or impossible to reach once the fence is assembled.
</Callout>

That last point matters in any season. The USDA Forest Products Laboratory names decay as the main cause of fence post failure, and says decay fungi need moisture to grow ([USDA FPL, *Protecting Wood Fences for Yard and Garden*](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/5700.pdf)). Gaps in the finish are where water gets in.

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>

### Can you install a wood fence in the rain?

Light rain usually doesn't stop post setting or hanging panels. Heavy rain, flooded post holes and saturated, muddy access are different, and a careful contractor will move the date. What rain really limits is **staining on site**, because stain labels require a dry window, for example no precipitation for 24 hours ([Flood](https://buyat.ppg.com/rep_pafpainttools_files/Flood/tdb/FLD802.pdf)).

### Does the ground freeze in Seattle in winter?

Not often for long. At Sea-Tac, days that stay at or below freezing average about 1.1 a year, and nights at or below 32°F average about 25 a year ([NOAA NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&format=pdf&stations=USW00024233&units=standard)). Colder, higher spots can differ. In most Puget Sound yards, water is a bigger winter issue than frost.

### What is the driest month to build a fence in Seattle?

July, with an average of 0.60 inches of precipitation and about 4.7 days of measurable rain at Sea-Tac, followed by August ([NOAA NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&format=pdf&stations=USW00024233&units=standard)).

### Can I stain a new cedar fence in winter?

Only on days that meet your product's label: typically a clean, dry surface, a set temperature range, and no rain for 24 hours. In Seattle those days are scarce from November through February. Pre-staining in a shop avoids the problem.

### Will a fence installed in winter last as long as one built in summer?

What decides how long a cedar fence lasts is mostly how it's built and protected: post details, ground contact, fasteners and finish. Our [wood fence lifespan guide](/blog/how-long-does-a-wood-fence-last-seattle) goes into it. A fence that goes up with every side already finished starts out protected, whatever month it's installed.

### Is winter a cheaper time to install a fence?

This guide doesn't cover pricing, because it depends on your yard, layout and style. Your [free quote](/quote) is the best way to see the real number for your project.
