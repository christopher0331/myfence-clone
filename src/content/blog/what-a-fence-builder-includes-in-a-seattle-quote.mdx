## Core Items Every Professional Fence Builder Quote Should Include

### Project Scope and Measurements

A professional **fence builder** starts with accurate measurements and a scope that matches your property, not a generic template.

Expect to see:

- Total **linear footage** of fence.
- Fence **height**.
- Fence **style** and material (e.g., cedar privacy, horizontal, chain link, ornamental metal).
- Number, type, and width of **gates**.
- Notes on slope, tight access, or obstacles (trees, roots, rock).

For inspiration on styles you might see in the quote, explore [/fence-styles](/fence-styles) before you request pricing.

### Materials: Lumber, Posts, Concrete, Hardware, Fasteners

Materials should be itemized, not bundled as a single line.

A thorough quote will spell out:

- **Wood species and grade** (e.g., Western Red Cedar vs. generic “cedar”).
- **Post type** and spacing (wood vs. steel, pressure treated, etc.).
- **Concrete** depth and amount per post.
- **Rails, pickets, and trim**.
- **Gate hardware** (hinges, latches, locks).
- **Fasteners**: At MyFence.com, we use in-house crews and **stainless fasteners** to handle Seattle’s wet climate and reduce rust and staining on your fence.

If a quote simply says “materials included” with no detail, you have no way to know what quality level you’re buying.

### Labor and Crew

Quality fences are about workmanship as much as materials. Your quote should clarify:

- Labor cost or total installed price.
- That work is done by **in-house crews** (like MyFence.com) or subcontractors.
- Estimated **timeline** for installation.
- Cleanup: hauling away debris, leveling disturbed soil, and removing old fence sections.

A professional **Seattle fence builder** will be clear about who is onsite and how long the crew expects to be there.

### Demo, Haul-Away, and Site Prep

If you have an existing fence, demo and removal should be explicitly listed:

- Removal of old fence (by linear foot).
- Haul-away and disposal fees.
- Removal of concrete footings if needed.
- Brush clearing or minor grading, if included.

Surprise demo charges are common when these items aren’t spelled out in the original quote.

### Gates, Latches, and Special Features

Gates are more complex than straight fence runs, and your quote should treat them that way.

Look for:

- Number of **gates**.
- Gate **width** (walk vs. driveway).
- Gate **style** (framed, metal frame, double gate).
- Hardware type (standard latch, lockable latch, self-closing, etc.).
- Any add-ons like **privacy screens** or custom trim.

When comparing two Seattle fence builder quotes, make sure you’re looking at the same gate count and type on each bid.

## Permits, Codes, and Utility Locates in Seattle

A professional fence builder doesn’t guess about local rules—they reference them and build within code.

### Fence Permits and Height Rules

In Seattle, many standard residential fences do not require a building permit, but there are important limits on height and location. The city’s official guidance is here:  
`https://www.seattle.gov/construction-and-inspections/permits/common-projects/fences`.

Your quote should note:

- Whether a permit is anticipated.
- Who handles permit paperwork if needed.
- Any height restrictions relevant to your lot (especially on slopes or near retaining walls).

For broader Washington fence permit context, see state-level building guidance at:  
`https://www.doh.wa.gov/CommunityandEnvironment/ConstructionandDevelopment/BuildingSafety`  
(look for local building and safety information and links to city permitting).

### Utility Locates – Call Before You Dig

Any professional **Seattle fence builder** will call for utility locates before digging post holes. In Washington, you can request locates through **Call 811**:

- Washington 811 information:  
  `https://www.commerce.wa.gov/811-washington-call-before-you-dig/`

Your quote might say “utility locates included” or list it as a separate step. If there’s no mention of utilities, that’s a safety and liability red flag.

## Licensing, Insurance, and Consumer Protection

A fence quote should do more than list numbers—it should reassure you that the contractor is properly licensed and insured.

### Contractor Licensing in Washington

You can verify your **Seattle fence builder** is properly registered and bonded with **Washington State Department of Labor & Industries (L&I)**:

- Contractor verification:  
  `https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/`

A professional quote will include:

- Company legal name and license number.
- Proof of liability insurance and, ideally, workers’ compensation coverage.

If the fence builder won’t provide a license number, you can treat that as a major warning sign.

### Consumer Protection Resources

To understand your rights when hiring any home improvement contractor in Washington:

- Washington Attorney General – Consumer Protection:  
  `https://www.atg.wa.gov/consumer-protection`
- Federal Trade Commission – Hiring a contractor advice:  
  `https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/articles/hiring-contractor`

A reputable **Seattle fence builder** will align with these best practices: written contracts, clear scopes, and transparent change-order processes.

### Local Code and Property Considerations

Fence location, height, and design can be affected by zoning and municipal code in Seattle:

- Seattle Municipal Code (Land Use & Zoning):  
  `https://library.municode.com/wa/seattle/codes/municipal_code`

Your quote may reference property lines, setbacks, or unique conditions like corner lots or steep slopes. If you’re in a specific neighborhood or hillside, check [/service-areas](/service-areas) to confirm MyFence.com coverage and typical local considerations.

## How to Compare Fence Builder Quotes in Seattle

When you have two or three bids from different fence builders, you want a fair, objective way to compare them.

### Line-Item Comparison Checklist

Walk through each quote and compare:

- **Fence length and height**: Same measurements on each?
- **Materials**: Same species, grade, post type, and fastener quality?
- **Gates**: Same count, width, and hardware?
- **Demo and disposal**: Included or extra?
- **Concrete and post depth**: Spelled out or vague?
- **Cleanup**: Will they haul debris and leave the yard tidy?
- **Warranty**: Written warranty on workmanship and, if applicable, materials?

If one quote is significantly cheaper, look closely at where the difference comes from—often it’s materials, depth of posts, fasteners, or omitted line items.

## Red Flags in Vague or Problematic Fence Quotes

Not every bid reflects professional standards. Watch for these warning signs before you commit:

### One-Number Quotes With No Details

If the quote is just “$X installed” with no breakdown of materials, gates, or demo, you have no way to compare it or hold the builder accountable.

### “Materials Included” Without Specs

Without specifics on lumber grade, post type, concrete depth, and fastener quality, a fence builder can use the cheapest option and still technically meet the quote.

In Seattle’s rainy climate, inferior fasteners or improperly treated wood show up quickly as leaning panels, rust, or premature rot.

### No Mention of Licensing or Insurance

If the quote doesn’t list a license number and the contractor isn’t easily verifiable using L&I’s tool at `https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/`, tread very carefully.

### No Discussion of Utilities or Property Lines

Professional fence builders will mention utility locates (Call 811) and property boundaries. If the quote ignores both, you risk damaging utilities or building over the line—issues that can lead to disputes and even legal action.

Seattle’s municipal code and land-use rules are available at  
`https://library.municode.com/wa/seattle/codes/municipal_code`  
and are one more reason your **Seattle fence builder** should be precise about layout.

### Cash-Only, No Written Contract

Consumer protection agencies including the Washington Attorney General (`https://www.atg.wa.gov/consumer-protection`) and FTC (`https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/articles/hiring-contractor`) strongly recommend written contracts for work like fencing. If a builder resists putting things in writing, that’s a major red flag.
