Here are the top choices for **wood fences in Seattle**:\n\n### Western Red Cedar: The PNW Favorite\n\nWithout a doubt, **Western Red Cedar** is the reigning champion for **cedar fence PNW** installations. Native to the region, it boasts natural properties that make it exceptionally well-suited for our climate.\n\n*   **Natural Rot and Insect Resistance:** Cedar contains natural oils (thujaplicins) that act as fungicides and insecticides, protecting it from decay and pests without chemical treatment.\n*   **Dimensional Stability:** Cedar is less prone to warping, shrinking, or swelling compared to many other wood types, maintaining its shape and integrity over time.\n*   **Lightweight and Workable:** Its low density makes it easy to work with during **fencing installation**, reducing labor costs.\n*   **Aesthetic Appeal:** Known for its rich, aromatic scent and beautiful reddish-brown hues that naturally weather to a silvery-grey patina if left unstained.\n\n### Pressure-Treated Pine\n\nPressure-treated pine is a popular, more budget-friendly alternative. It's typically Southern Yellow Pine infused with chemical preservatives under high pressure to resist rot, fungi, and insects.\n\n*   **Cost-Effective:** Generally less expensive upfront than cedar.\n*   **Durability:** The treatment process significantly extends its lifespan, making it suitable for ground contact.\n*   **Paintable/Stainable:** Accepts stains and paints well, allowing for customization.\n\n<Callout title=\"Real-world failure\">\nWhile pressure-treated pine is durable, it can be prone to warping and cracking if not properly dried and maintained. However, it lacks the natural rot and insect resistance of cedar.\n\n*   **Strong and Sturdy:** Excellent structural integrity.\n*   **Cost-Effective:** Often more affordable than cedar.\n*   **Requires Treatment:** Must be treated with preservatives or regularly sealed and stained to withstand the PNW moisture and resist decay.\n\n#### Wood Type Comparison for Seattle Fences\n\n| Feature | Western Red Cedar | Pressure-Treated Pine | Douglas Fir |
