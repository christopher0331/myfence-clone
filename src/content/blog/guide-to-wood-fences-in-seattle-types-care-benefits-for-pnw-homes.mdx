## Understanding Wood Fence Types for the PNW Climate

Choosing the right wood is crucial, especially with Seattle's unique weather patterns. Here are the most common **wood fence types** suited for the region:

### Cedar Fences: The PNW Favorite
**Cedar fence PNW** is the undisputed champion for its natural resistance to rot, decay, and insect infestation. Western Red Cedar, in particular, is highly sought after for its beautiful grain, aromatic scent, and ability to withstand moisture without warping. It weathers to a lovely silver-gray if left untreated or can be stained to maintain its rich reddish-brown hue.

### Pressure-Treated Pine
Pressure-treated pine is a popular, more budget-friendly option. It undergoes a chemical treatment process that makes it resistant to rot, fungi, and insects. While not as naturally resilient as cedar, it offers good durability when properly maintained. It typically requires regular staining or painting to protect it from the elements.

### Redwood
While less common in the PNW due to cost and availability, redwood is another excellent choice known for its natural resistance to rot and insects. It boasts a stunning rich color and exceptional durability, making it a premium option.

<Callout title="Important" variant="info">
When sourcing wood for your fence in Seattle, always prioritize sustainably harvested timber. Local suppliers often have the best understanding of environmentally responsible options.
</Callout>

Here's a quick comparison of common wood types:

| Feature | Western Red Cedar | Pressure-Treated Pine |

## Essential Wood Fence Maintenance for PNW Weather

The key to a long-lasting **wood fence Seattle** is consistent **wood fence maintenance**. Seattle's damp climate can be tough on wood, so proactive care is essential.

1.  **Regular Cleaning**: Annually, clean your fence to remove moss, mildew, and dirt. A gentle pressure wash or scrubbing with a mild detergent and brush works wonders. Be careful not to use too high a pressure, as it can damage the wood fibers.
    <Callout title="Real-world failure">
    Using too high a pressure washer setting can strip wood fibers, making your fence more susceptible to water damage and rot. Always start with a low setting and increase gradually.
    </Callout>
2.  **Inspect for Damage**: Periodically check for loose boards, nails, screws, or signs of rot and insect damage. Address minor repairs promptly to prevent them from escalating. Pay close attention to the bottom of the fence where it meets the ground.
3.  **Staining or Sealing**: This is perhaps the most critical step for **wood fence maintenance** in the PNW.
    *   **Stain**: A good quality wood stain protects against UV rays, moisture, and provides a beautiful finish. Semi-transparent stains allow the wood grain to show through, while solid stains offer more color and protection.
    *   **Sealer**: A clear wood sealer provides a protective barrier against moisture without changing the wood's natural color.
    Reapply stain or sealer every 2-5 years, depending on the product and exposure.
    <Callout title="Pro tip" variant="success">
    Before staining or sealing your **cedar fence PNW**, ensure the wood is completely dry. Applying product to damp wood can trap moisture, leading to peeling or mildew growth.
    </Callout>
4.  **Trim Vegetation**: Keep plants, bushes, and trees trimmed away from your fence. This prevents moisture retention against the wood, reduces insect access, and allows for better air circulation, which is vital in our humid climate.
5.  **Address Drainage**: Ensure proper drainage around the base of your fence to prevent water from pooling and accelerating rot, especially at the fence posts.
