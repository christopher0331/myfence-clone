## Tehaleh at a glance (why fences are reviewed here)

Tehaleh is a **~4,700-acre master-planned community** in east Pierce County, planned for thousands of homes, large open-space corridors, and long-term build-out under Brookfield / NASH partnerships. Official community materials and regional planning pages describe it as a mixed-use community immediately south of Bonney Lake where South Puget Sound meets the Cascade foothills:

- Community overview: [Tehaleh.com](https://www.tehaleh.com/) and [Tehaleh Community](https://www.tehaleh.com/community/)
- Growth vision: [Tehaleh Future Plans](https://www.tehaleh.com/future-plans/)
- Background story: [This is the Story of Tehaleh](https://www.tehaleh.com/life-at-tehaleh/blog/posts-by-date/2019/february/this-is-the-story-of-tehaleh/)
- Developer context: [NASH Communities — Tehaleh](https://www.nashcommunities.com/projects/tehaleh) and [Brookfield Residential — Tehaleh](https://www.brookfieldresidential.com/washington/seattle/tehaleh)
- County planning: [Pierce County — Tehaleh](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/4183/Tehaleh) and [Tehaleh Regulations](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/4195/Regulations)

In communities like this, fencing is not just a backyard upgrade — it is part of a coordinated streetscape. That is why Design Review looks at **height, materials, color, placement, and trail/open-space adjacency** before you build.

## What Tehaleh’s “Meet Cohere” update means for homeowners

Tehaleh’s March 2025 blog — **[There’s a New HOA in Town: Meet Cohere](https://www.tehaleh.com/life-at-tehaleh/blog/posts-by-date/2025/march/theres-a-new-hoa-in-town-meet-cohere/)** — explains that Cohere is taking on association management with a resident-first approach:

- **Local access:** Cohere’s Tehaleh team highlights an on-site presence on **Myers Road**, with open office hours for residents (weekdays, roughly 9:00 a.m.–4:30 p.m. per the community post).
- **Transparency:** Open board meetings and clearer communication channels.
- **Resident hub:** One place for e-blasts, newsletters, and association information instead of scattered platforms.
- **Tone:** Less “ruler-wielding enforcer,” more stewardship, engagement, and shared standards that keep Tehaleh coherent as it grows.

Cohere specializes in supporting master-planned communities — see [Cohere](https://cohere.life/) and [Cohere communities](https://cohere.life/communities/). For fence projects, that usually means a clearer path to the current Design Review packet, contacts, and submission expectations.

## How fence Design Review usually works (and how we help)

Industry education from the [Community Associations Institute (CAI)](https://www.caionline.org/) describes why associations regulate exterior changes: aesthetics, property values, and consistent community character. Washington homeowners can also review state common-interest statutes such as [RCW 64.38 (Homeowners’ Associations)](https://apps.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=64.38) and [RCW 64.90 (Washington Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act)](https://apps.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=64.90), plus consumer resources from the [Washington Attorney General’s Consumer Protection](https://www.atg.wa.gov/consumer-protection) office.

A typical Tehaleh-ready fencing path looks like this:

1. **Confirm your phase** — Upper Tehaleh, Lower Tehaleh, Trilogy, and Discovery Park can differ slightly on placement and style. Start with our neighborhood pages for [Upper Tehaleh](/service-areas/bonney-lake/upper-tehaleh) and [Lower Tehaleh](/service-areas/bonney-lake/lower-tehaleh).
2. **Gather HOA-friendly specs** — Earth-tone stains, no chain-link shortcuts, site plan, elevations, materials, and color samples. Download the [Tehaleh HOA Fence Submission Checklist](/docs/hoa/tehaleh-hoa-fence-submission-checklist.pdf).
3. **Submit through association management** — Request the current Design Review forms via Cohere / Tehaleh Owner’s Association, then file a complete package. Walk-in questions at the Myers Road office can help if something is unclear.
4. **Wait for written approval** — Do not dig post holes until approval is in writing (including any conditions).
5. **Install exactly to the approved plan** — Wind-ready builds on the plateau, trail-smart setbacks in Lower Tehaleh, and finishes that match what reviewers already signed off on.

## HOA approval is not the same as a county (or city) permit

Tehaleh sits in **unincorporated Pierce County**, immediately south of the City of Bonney Lake. That means you may need to satisfy **two layers**:

- **Association Design Review** (Tehaleh / Cohere)
- **Pierce County** land-use and building rules where they apply — start with [Pierce County Tehaleh](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/4183/Tehaleh) and [Tehaleh Regulations](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/4195/Regulations)

If you are comparing notes with neighbors inside Bonney Lake city limits, also check:

- [City of Bonney Lake](https://www.ci.bonney-lake.wa.us/)
- [Bonney Lake Permit Center](https://www.ci.bonney-lake.wa.us/260/Permit-Center)
- [Community Development](https://www.ci.bonney-lake.wa.us/161/Community-Development)
- [Bonney Lake Municipal Code](https://www.codepublishing.com/WA/Bonneylake/)

<Callout title="Real-world failure">
Homeowners sometimes get HOA approval, then discover a setback, easement, or height issue under county/city rules — or the reverse. Build only after both association approval and any required government permits are clear.
</Callout>
